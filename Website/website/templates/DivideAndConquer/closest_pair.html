Closest Pair of Points

Given $n$ points in the plane, find the pair with the smallest distance between them. Checking every pair is $O(n^2)$; the divide & conquer solution does it in $O(n \log n)$ — sweep the plane with a recursive split, then settle the only thing the halves can't see: pairs that straddle the dividing line.

Divide, Conquer, and the δ-Strip

  1. Sort once, two ways: by $x$ (for the split) and by $y$ (for the strip scan). Both orders are maintained through the recursion.
  2. Divide at the median $x$; conquer each half recursively. The better of the two results is $\delta = \min(d_Q, d_R)$ — but a closer pair might still cross the line.
  3. Strip check: only points within $\delta$ of the dividing line can matter, and within that strip (scanned bottom-up in $y$ order) a point only needs to look at neighbours less than $\delta$ above it. Geometry does the rest: points on one side are pairwise $\ge \delta$ apart, so a $\delta \times 2\delta$ window around any strip point holds at most a constant number of candidates — the classic bound is 7 neighbours — making the whole strip pass linear time.

What the animation shows: 35 scattered points (a fixed random seed, so your run matches the video). Verified result: on this exact point set the closest pair is the two points near (-3.75, 0.74) and (-3.94, 0.94) at distance 0.28 (0.2767 before rounding) — the scene's divide & conquer run and an independent $O(n^2)$ all-pairs check agree.

Complexity

  • Best / Worst / Average: $\Theta(n \log n)$ — the combine step is $O(n)$ (building the strip is a filtered pass over the $y$-sorted list, and the packed-window argument caps each point's strip comparisons at a constant), so $T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n)$ — master theorem case 2 with $a = 2$, $b = 2$, $f(n) = O(n) = O(n^{\log_b a})$ gives $\Theta(n \log n)$, matching the $\Theta(n \log n)$ up-front sorts. The recursion shape is fixed, so no case behaves differently.
  • Space: $\Theta(n)$ — the two sorted copies flow through the recursion (each level re-partitions them in $O(n)$ scratch), plus a $\Theta(\log n)$ recursion stack.

Must know: the strip scan must run in $y$ order — that's what makes the "look upward until the gap exceeds δ" break valid; both sorted orders are load-bearing (an $x$-sorted strip scan would be $O(n^2)$ again); base cases of 2 or 3 points are brute-forced; and if the two closest points lie in different halves, the strip is the only place they can be found — that's the whole correctness argument.

Applications

  • Collision detection: a proximity pre-pass before fine-grained checks.
  • Robotics & path planning: nearest obstacle pairs define the tightest corridors.
  • Air-traffic monitoring: proximity alerts between tracked aircraft.
  • Computational geometry: the engine inside mesh simplification and nearest-neighbour preprocessing.

Three Ways to Solve It

Approach Time Guarantee
All pairs $\Theta(n^2)$ simple, exact
Divide & conquer (this page) $\Theta(n \log n)$ worst-case
Randomized grid hashing (Rabin) $O(n)$ expected probabilistic

More divide & conquer: Count Inversions (the same two-halves-plus-linear-combine shape) and Median of Medians (uneven halves that still work).
